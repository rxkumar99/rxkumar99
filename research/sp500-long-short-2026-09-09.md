The regime did not rotate. It escalated.

  began in late February. Gasoline $4.429.
- **10-year Treasury 4.79%**, up from 4.20% at the start of 2026.
- **The Fed is now debating a hike, not a cut.** It held at 3.50–3.75% on 29 July with
  *three dissents* wanting an increase; Governor Barr said the Fed "should act decisively to
  raise" if inflation doesn't turn. Futures price a **58% chance of a September hike**.
- **Qatar's Ras Laffan** — the world's largest LNG hub — is out **12.8 mtpa on a 3-to-5-year
  repair timeline**. LNG volumes through Hormuz are down 95%.

So Book II is the same logic aimed at a sharper regime: long the energy shock and the things
it pays for, short the balance sheets that have to absorb it.

## Risks

- **This is one bet, not ten.** Long COP and LNG, short AAL, CCL and SYY is the same oil view
  expressed five times — roughly half the gross. Unlike the August book, that concentration is
  deliberate and is the thesis. A Hormuz reopening or a ceasefire hits five positions at once,
  in the same direction, on the same day. Size accordingly.
- **AAL is a solvency short, not a valuation short.** At 2.99x leverage and near-breakeven
  earnings it is the most volatile position here in both directions. Any fuel relief produces
  a violent squeeze off a $12.97 base.
- **A Fed hike is 58% priced, not certain.** If September passes without one and yields fall,
  PHM and CHTR both re-rate up together.
- **AVGO is the only high-multiple long.** It is uncorrelated to the rest of the book, which
  helps, but it carries the book's single-name event risk at the next print.
- **NOC's discount may be deserved.** A $518 price against a $774 high implies a
  program-execution problem the war does not fix. This is the position most likely to be a
  value trap rather than a value.
- **Dividend carry is small this time.** CHTR and AAL pay nothing; CCL is negligible. Short
  SYY (~2.7%) and PHM (~0.7%) are the only meaningful drags — well under the August book's
  ~$800/yr.
